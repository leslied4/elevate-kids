FINANCIAL STEWARDS • CHAPTER 2
Scene 1

The Closed Hand

Saturday morning...
If I give any of my money away, I’ll have less for me!
Kofi has started learning stewardship—but fear is still whispering: “Hold on tight. You might not have enough.”
What if trusting God means opening your hand instead of squeezing everything?
Ama remembers the lesson: God is not presented as someone trying to take everything away. The chapter teaches that God is a giver and the source of blessing.
CLOSED HAND THINKING
“Everything must stay with me or I won’t have enough.”
OPEN HAND THINKING
“God is my source. I can give, save, spend, and share as a faithful steward.”
Big idea: Godly prosperity is not the same as greed. The lesson contrasts hoarding for yourself with receiving God’s blessings and becoming a blessing to others.
2
Bible Adventure

David Prepares for God’s House

1 Chronicles 29
I want to prepare gold, silver, and materials for the house of God.
David set aside enormous resources for the temple that Solomon would build. Then he personally gave even more and invited the leaders to give willingly too.
David’s attitude mattered as much as the size of the gift.
“O LORD our God, all this store that we have prepared... cometh of thine hand, and is all thine own.” 1 Chronicles 29:16
David understood stewardship: “We are giving back from what You first gave us.” That is very different from thinking God is poor and needs us to rescue Him.
3
Heart Check

God Wants the Heart, Not a Performance

Psalm 50 shows that God did not need Israel’s animals as though He lacked something. Everything already belonged to Him.
The lesson says giving helps people express trust, dependence, gratitude, and the recognition that God is their source.
“For every beast of the forest is mine, and the cattle upon a thousand hills.” Psalm 50:10
Important: Giving is not a way to buy God’s love. God already owns everything. Giving trains the heart to trust Him and remember who the source is.
4
Scene 2

Fear Says “What If?” — Faith Says “God Is My Source”

What if I lose what I have? What if there isn’t enough?
The lesson describes how stressful money becomes when we think every outcome depends completely on us.
A steward still plans and works—but doesn’t have to pretend she controls everything.
Seeing God as the source gives the lesson’s promised benefit: peace of mind and security beyond changing circumstances.
“But my God shall supply all your need according to his riches in glory by Christ Jesus.” Philippians 4:19
5
Remember the Source

Who Gave You the Ability?

I studied hard and worked hard!
Yes—and who gave you life, abilities, opportunities, strength, and people who helped you?
The lesson does not deny work. It teaches humility: even our power to produce, earn, learn, build, and succeed is something we received.
“But thou shalt remember the LORD thy God: for it is he that giveth thee power to get wealth...” Deuteronomy 8:18
“...what hast thou that thou didst not receive?” 1 Corinthians 4:7
Why blessing? Not merely so we can collect more things. The chapter connects blessing with becoming a blessing—having enough to participate in good works and help others.
6
Steward Skills

Giving Does Not Cancel Wisdom

Pause before impulse buying. Wanting something now is not the same as needing it.
Prepare. Stewardship includes setting money aside instead of using everything immediately.
Think ahead. The lesson warns against debt and paying far more than an item’s real value because of interest.
A giver can also be a saver. Giving, saving, planning, and wise spending all fit together under stewardship. The opposite of generosity is not saving—it is selfishness and fear.
7
Kingdom Thinking

Blessed to Be a Blessing

I have an extra notebook. You can have it.
Thank you! I needed one!
Children may not have salaries, but they can already learn the pattern: work, receive, manage, share, and help.
Ephesians 4:28 and 2 Corinthians 9:8 connect work and sufficiency with having something to give and abound in good works.
“...let him labour, working with his hands the thing which is good, that he may have to give to him that needeth.” Ephesians 4:28
“And God is able to make all grace abound toward you... that ye... may abound to every good work.” 2 Corinthians 9:8
8
Watch Your Heart

Prosperity Is Not the Same as Loving Money

The chapter warns against wanting riches as the goal of life. Scripture says the love of money brings temptation and sorrow.
The lesson’s alternative is not poverty as a goal. It is receiving and managing blessing without making money your master.
“The blessing of the LORD, it maketh rich, and he addeth no sorrow with it.” Proverbs 10:22
“For the love of money is the root of all evil...” 1 Timothy 6:10
Heart check: Do I want money mainly so I can feel important, own more than everyone else, or control everything? Or do I see money as something to manage under God?
9
Scene 3

Seek First the Kingdom

So my goal isn’t just to “get rich”?
Right. Your first pursuit is God and His kingdom.
And what He gives you can help you do good!
This is the chapter’s direction: God first. Stewardship follows. Money becomes a servant of God’s purposes instead of the ruler of your heart.
“But seek ye first the kingdom of God, and his righteousness; and all these things shall be added unto you.” Matthew 6:33
The two big steps from Lesson 2:
1. Recognize that God is your source.
2. Develop the mindset of a steward.
10
The Big Idea

Kofi Opens His Hand

I don’t have to choose between generosity and wisdom. A steward does both!
Kofi is learning that an open hand is not careless. It is generous, thoughtful, and free from fear.
That is the rhythm of financial stewardship in this chapter.
Chapter 2 in one sentence:
Because God is the source of what we have, we can trust Him, give willingly, plan wisely, avoid greed, and use His blessings to become a blessing to others.
