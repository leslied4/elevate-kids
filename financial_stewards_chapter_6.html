FINANCIAL STEWARDS • CHAPTER 6
Scene 1

What Will You Do With What You Have?

God gave me something. Now what?
Lesson 6 begins with the parable of the talents: servants were entrusted with different amounts and expected to do something useful with what they received.
The lesson teaches that God expects us to use what He gives, not simply hide it.
“...I was afraid, and went and hid thy talent in the earth...” Matthew 25:25
2
Bible Adventure

Five, Two, and One

The servant with five talents multiplied what had been entrusted to him.
The servant with two talents did the same.
The third servant acted out of fear and buried his talent.
Stewardship means action: use what God entrusts to you faithfully and productively.
3
People Matter

Resources Are Meant to Touch Lives

Right after the talents, Matthew 25 speaks about feeding the hungry, giving drink, clothing people, welcoming strangers, and visiting those in need. Lesson 6 connects these acts to stewardship.
“Inasmuch as ye have done it unto one of the least of these my brethren, ye have done it unto me.” Matthew 25:40
Why increase? Not just so “I have more,” but so “I can do more good.”
4
Heart Check

Prosperity Can Be Selfish—or Generous

Selfish prosperity:
“I want more so I can impress people, collect more stuff, and satisfy every desire.”
Generous prosperity:
“I want greater capacity so I can provide, give, serve, and help others.”
Lesson 6 repeatedly says that the motive behind giving is more important than merely looking generous.
5
Love First

Even a Huge Gift Can Miss the Point

The lesson uses 1 Corinthians 13:3 to show that even dramatic sacrifice is empty if love is absent.
Giving only to get something back is not the heart this lesson is teaching.
“...though I bestow all my goods to feed the poor... and have not charity, it profiteth me nothing.” 1 Corinthians 13:3
6
Money Isn't the Villain

The Love of Money Is the Problem

Lesson 6 says money itself is not evil.
The problem is trusting money, finding fulfillment in it, or loving what it can buy more than God.
“For the love of money is the root of all evil...” 1 Timothy 6:10
Important distinction: Having resources does not automatically make someone ungodly, and lacking resources does not automatically make someone more spiritual. The lesson focuses on the heart.
7
True Prosperity

How Much Can You Give?

Lesson 6 defines true prosperity by the ability to give and “abound to every good work,” not merely by possessions on paper.
“...having all sufficiency in all things, may abound to every good work.” 2 Corinthians 9:8
8
Prosperity Is Relative

It Doesn't Look the Same Everywhere

The lesson says prosperity is relative. It may look very different in different communities and countries.
The point is not a luxury standard; it is increase, sufficiency, and greater capacity to bless others.
9
Bible Adventure

Joseph Was Prosperous Before He Was Rich

How could the Bible call him prosperous?
Genesis 39 says “the LORD was with Joseph, and he was a prosperous man” even while he was a servant in Egypt.
Lesson 6 uses Joseph to show that prosperity is deeper than a bank balance.
“And the LORD was with Joseph, and he was a prosperous man...” Genesis 39:2
10
Joseph Keeps Serving

A Blessing Everywhere He Went

Joseph served faithfully in Potiphar's house, in prison, and eventually as second in command in Egypt. His position later helped save many people—including his own family—from famine.
The pattern: blessing others came before personal comfort.
11
Seed and Harvest

Isaac Sowed in a Famine

Plant now? In a famine?
Lesson 6 points to Isaac sowing during a difficult year and receiving a large harvest.
The lesson uses sowing as a picture for giving and trusting God rather than letting fear shut down generosity.
“Then Isaac sowed in that land, and received in the same year an hundredfold: and the LORD blessed him.” Genesis 26:12
12
Two Heart Conditions

Eaters and Sowers

EATER HEART:
“Everything that comes to me is mainly for me. I give only if something is left.”
SOWER HEART:
“I have needs too, but I look for opportunities to give and put God's kingdom first.”
These are the lesson's own terms for two dominant attitudes toward money.
“Now he that ministereth seed to the sower... multiply your seed sown...” 2 Corinthians 9:10
13
Growth Takes Time

Seeds Don't Become Trees Overnight

Lesson 6 says prosperity doesn't happen overnight. There is time between planting and harvest, and character must grow along with resources.
Child-friendly takeaway: generosity is a habit we practice over time. We do not give today because we expect a jackpot tomorrow.
14
The Big Idea

Prosperity Isn't Selfish

That is the chapter's central picture of prosperity.
Chapter 6 in one sentence:
Prosperity is not selfish when the heart is motivated by love and the increase God entrusts to us expands our ability to serve, give, provide, and bless other people.
15
Steward Challenge
