FINANCIAL STEWARDS • CHAPTER 3
Scene 1

What Is “Least”?

I thought money was a HUGE deal!
Jesus called faithfulness with money a “least” thing compared with true riches.
Lesson 3 starts in Luke 16. The point is not that money is unimportant—it is that money is smaller than the deeper things of God.
The lesson calls money “that which is least” and contrasts it with “true riches.”
“He that is faithful in that which is least is faithful also in much...” Luke 16:10
2
Jesus’ Teaching

Money Is a Servant, Not a Master

Jesus warned that no servant can serve two masters. The lesson identifies mammon with money and asks who really has our trust.
Financial stewardship is a way of practicing trust: money is managed, but God is trusted.
“Ye cannot serve God and mammon.” Luke 16:13
Chapter question: Am I using money—or is money using me?
3
Important Truth

You Cannot Buy a Miracle

The source lesson explicitly rejects this idea. Giving is not a purchase price for healing, salvation, or any blessing.
The lesson’s connection is about trust: practicing trust in smaller areas builds a pattern of trusting God more deeply.
Very important: God’s love is not for sale. Healing is not for sale. Salvation is not for sale. Giving is about trust, not payment.
4
Faith Practice

Don’t Divide Your Trust Into Boxes

Can I trust God in one part of life but refuse to trust Him in another?
The lesson says faith should not be compartmentalized. Trust grows when it is practiced consistently.
The central idea is consistency. If fear controls our financial decisions, that same fear can weaken confidence elsewhere.
5
Bible Adventure

The Rich Young Ruler

Mark 10:17–22
Good Master, what shall I do that I may inherit eternal life?
Why callest thou me good? There is none good but one, that is, God.
The young ruler appeared sincere. But Jesus looked beyond appearances to the heart.
The lesson interprets Jesus’ command as exposing where the man’s trust really was.
“Then Jesus beholding him loved him...” Mark 10:21
Notice the motive: Jesus loved the man. The confrontation was not cruelty; it was an invitation to move his trust from possessions to God.
6
Bible Adventure

The Hard Choice

Jesus told him to sell what he had, give to the poor, and follow Him.
His response revealed how strongly his heart was attached to his possessions.
The issue was not:
“Having possessions is automatically wrong.”
The issue was:
“Do possessions have your heart?”
“...go thy way, sell whatsoever thou hast, and give to the poor... and come... follow me.” Mark 10:21
7
A Helpful Contrast

Why Didn’t Jesus Tell Everyone the Same Thing?

Lesson 3 points out that Jesus did not tell every wealthy person to sell everything. Zacchaeus voluntarily chose generosity. The lesson uses this contrast to emphasize trust rather than a universal rule about owning things.
Ask yourself: If God asked me to release something I value, would fear control me—or could I trust Him?
8
Faith in Practice

Giving Shows What We Rely On

The lesson describes trust as reliance—where your confidence rests for support.
Giving doesn’t create God’s love. But it can reveal whether I believe He is my source.
That distinction is central to keeping this lesson healthy and clear for children.
“Give, and it shall be given unto you...” Luke 6:38
9
Growing Faith

Small Trust Can Train Us for Greater Trust

Faith isn’t a magic ladder where I earn bigger blessings...
...but practicing trust in everyday choices can help me become steadier when harder things come.
That captures the chapter’s child-friendly takeaway without turning faith into a transaction.
Growth takes practice. A child who learns to trust God with allowance, possessions, time, and generosity is learning a pattern of dependence that can carry into bigger decisions later.
10
The Whole Life

God Wants More Than One Hour on Sunday

Lesson 3 says God does not want to be limited to “church time.” Financial choices are part of discipleship because they are part of real life.
Chapter 3 in one sentence:
Faithfulness with money is not a way to purchase God’s blessings; it is one practical way to learn consistent trust in God so that our whole lives—not just one part—rest on Him.
11
Steward Challenge

Talk, Think & Do

🧠 Think About It

1. According to Luke 16, what does the lesson call “that which is least”?
2. What are “true riches” compared with money?
3. Can giving money buy a miracle? Why not?
4. What did the rich young ruler’s response reveal about his heart?
5. Why is Zacchaeus an important contrast?
6. What does it mean to rely on God instead of relying on money?

✋ Faithfulness Challenge

Choose one “small” area this week where you can practice trust and faithfulness—your allowance, your time, your belongings, or something you promised to do. Be faithful there.

“God, help me trust You in the small things and the greater things.”
