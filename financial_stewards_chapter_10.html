FINANCIAL STEWARDS • CHAPTER 10
Final Chapter

What Does It Mean to Trust God?

Can I say I trust God in prayer but keep finances completely separate?
Lesson 10 ties the whole course together: trust is meant to include every area of life, including money.
The lesson centers Proverbs 3:5–10 and connects trusting God with honoring Him through firstfruits.
“Trust in the LORD with all thine heart; and lean not unto thine own understanding.” Proverbs 3:5
2
All of Life

Don't Split Life Into “Spiritual” and “Everyday”

Lesson 10 rejects compartmentalizing life. The same trust brought to prayer should also shape work, money, and daily responsibilities.
Final-chapter question: Is God only part of my Sunday—or part of how I actually live?
3
Firstfruits Again

Trust Shows Up in What Comes First

Lesson 10 repeats the firstfruits principle from earlier chapters.
In the lesson, putting giving first is one practical way of acting on trust.
“Honour the LORD with thy substance, and with the firstfruits of all thine increase.” Proverbs 3:9
4
Seed Wisdom

Don't Eat Tomorrow's Seed Today

Lesson 10 returns to the seed picture: some resources meet today's needs, while some are planted toward the future.
Wisdom: taking care of today matters, but consuming absolutely everything leaves nothing for generosity or tomorrow.
5
Generosity

Scatter—and Still Increase?

Giving away and increasing sounds backwards!
Proverbs 11:24–25 is used in the lesson to describe generosity in God's economy.
The lesson describes the generous person as someone who will also be refreshed.
“There is that scattereth, and yet increaseth...” Proverbs 11:24
6
Bible Adventure

The Widow's Two Mites

It doesn't look like much...
Jesus said she gave more than all the others.
Jesus watched people give at the temple treasury. A poor widow put in two tiny coins.
Lesson 10 uses her story to show that generosity is relative to what a person has, not merely the size of the number.
“...this poor widow hath cast in more than they all.” Luke 21:3
7
Don't Judge by Appearances

You Can't See Generosity by Looking at Stuff

Lesson 10 warns against judging someone's generosity only by what they appear to own.
The lesson's standard is seed and generosity, not appearance alone.
For children: don't decide whether someone is generous just by looking at their clothes, house, or possessions.
8
Trust vs. Riches

What Holds You Up?

Money can become a false foundation if we treat it as our ultimate security.
Proverbs 11:28 provides the final image: the righteous flourishing like a branch.
“He that trusteth in his riches shall fall: but the righteous shall flourish as a branch.” Proverbs 11:28
9
Sowing and Reaping

What You Sow Matters

Lesson 10 returns to Galatians 6:7 and Luke 6:38, stressing not only sowing but the way a person gives.
“...whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap.” Galatians 6:7
10
The Whole Book

Ten Lessons, One Stewardship Story

Chapter 10 functions as the capstone: heart, trust, generosity, wisdom, partnership, and stewardship all come together.
11
The Big Idea

In God We Trust

The final image is not a pile of money. It is a flourishing life rooted in trust.
Chapter 10 in one sentence:
Financial stewardship reaches its goal when money stops being our source of security and becomes a tool we manage while our trust, identity, and confidence rest in God.
12
Final Steward Challenge

Talk, Think & Do

🧠 Think About It

1. How does Proverbs 3 connect trusting God with firstfruits?
2. Why does Lesson 10 reject separating “spiritual life” from money and work?
3. What does the seed picture teach about today and tomorrow?
4. Why did Jesus say the widow gave more than the rich people?
5. Why shouldn't we judge generosity by appearances?
6. What is the difference between trusting riches and flourishing like a branch?
7. Which of the ten stewardship lessons do you most want to practice?

🌳 Final Steward Challenge

Make your own simple stewardship plan with a parent or church teacher. When you receive money or another resource, decide how you will give, save, spend wisely, and use something to bless another person.

“God, everything I have comes from You. Teach me to manage it faithfully.”
