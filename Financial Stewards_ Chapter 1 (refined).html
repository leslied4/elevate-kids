FINANCIAL STEWARDS • CHAPTER 1
Scene 1

Mine... or entrusted to me?

Sunday afternoon, after church...
Uncle gave me $20! I can buy whatever I want!
Kofi is excited. In his mind, the money is completely his, so the only question is what he wants to do with it.
But what if God gave it to you to manage wisely?
Ama asks a question that changes the whole conversation.
At kids’ Bible class...
A steward takes care of something for its owner. Scripture teaches us to think that way about what God gives us.
Key idea: Our money, abilities, opportunities, and other good gifts are things God has entrusted to us. A financial steward asks, “How should I use this well?”
“Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above...” James 1:17
2
Scene 2

From Owner Thinking to Steward Thinking

“It is all mine. I must handle everything.”
When we see ourselves as the ultimate source of everything, money can start to feel like a heavy burden.
“God is my source. I will manage faithfully.”
A steward still makes responsible choices, but remembers that provision, ability, and opportunity ultimately come from God.
Teacher Miriam explains...
That means I can ask God what to do instead of grabbing everything for myself!
This change of mindset—from owner to steward—is the foundation of the lesson.
Remember: Being a steward does not mean doing nothing. It means working faithfully while recognizing that God gives the ability, opportunity, increase, and resources.
3
Bible Adventure

Abraham Learns to Trust the Source

Genesis 12
Go to the land I will show you. I will bless you... and you will be a blessing.
God called Abram to leave what was familiar and trust Him. God promised to bless him and make him a blessing to others.
Abraham worked, but he did not see his own effort as the ultimate source of his blessing.
The lesson emphasizes that Abraham’s prosperity was connected to God’s promise and favor—not merely cleverness or effort.
“...I will bless thee, and make thy name great; and thou shalt be a blessing.” Genesis 12:2
BLESSED → TO BE A BLESSING
4
Bible Adventure

Lot Chooses First

Genesis 13
Lot, you choose first. If you go one way, I’ll go the other.
Then I choose the green, well-watered land!
Their herds had grown so large that there was not enough land for both groups. Abraham let Lot choose first.
Lot chose the land that looked naturally better.
Abraham could afford to be generous because his confidence was in God, not simply in the appearance of the land.
After Lot separated from Abram, the LORD told Abram to look in every direction and promised the land to him and his descendants. Genesis 13:14–17
5
Bible Adventure

“Let No One Say He Made Me Rich”

Genesis 14
Keep the goods for yourself!
After Abraham rescued Lot and defeated the invading kings, the king of Sodom offered Abraham the recovered goods.
No. I will not take it, so you cannot say, “I made Abram rich.”
Abraham wanted it to be clear that the king was not the source of his prosperity.
“I will not take from a thread even to a shoelatchet... lest thou shouldest say, I have made Abram rich.” Genesis 14:22–23
The heart of the lesson: Abraham’s confidence in God was stronger than his desire to grab every possible financial advantage. He could release what was offered because he believed God was his source.
6
Bible Adventure

God Is the Reward

After Abraham refused to make the king of Sodom his source, God spoke to him in a vision.
A clenched-fist attitude makes possessions the center of our confidence.
Stewardship begins when we recognize that God—not money—is our source and security.
“Fear not, Abram: I am thy shield, and thy exceeding great reward.” Genesis 15:1
7
Back to the Kids

A Steward Still Works!

So trusting God doesn’t mean I sit around and wait?
No. The lesson uses a farmer as the example: a farmer prepares the soil and plants the seed.
He works faithfully, but he cannot manufacture the sun, rain, soil, health, or life in the seed.
Work matters. But the steward recognizes that even the ability to work and the conditions for increase are gifts.
I can work hard...
...make wise choices...
...and still remember God is my source!
Exactly. Stewardship is not laziness. It is faithful responsibility without pretending we created every good thing by ourselves.
8
The Big Idea

Kofi Makes a Steward Plan

Maybe “mine” isn’t the best first word. First I should ask, “How can I be faithful with this?”
Kofi does not have to spend nothing. The change is that he now thinks like a steward before he acts.
The goal is not to fear money or worship money, but to put it in its proper place.
Chapter 1 in one sentence:
A financial steward works faithfully and manages wisely, while recognizing that God is the source of every good gift and that what we receive should be used according to His purposes.
“For in him we live, and move, and have our being...” Acts 17:28
