FINANCIAL STEWARDS • CHAPTER 4
Scene 1

Where Is Your Treasure?

Treasure means gold and money, right?
Kofi imagines treasure as coins in a chest. But Jesus’ teaching goes deeper: treasure points to what our hearts value and trust.
Lesson 4 begins with this principle: where money goes can reveal what matters most to us.
“Lay not up for yourselves treasures upon earth... But lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven... For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also.” Matthew 6:19–21
2
Heart Check

Having Money Is Not the Same as Trusting Money

Lesson 4 does not say money itself is evil. It warns about putting confidence in what money can do.
The danger is letting money become the thing that makes us feel safe, powerful, or important.
“Children, how hard is it for them that trust in riches to enter into the kingdom of God!” Mark 10:24
The key word is trust. The lesson’s warning is not simply “rich people are bad.” It is about where confidence rests.
3
Bible Adventure

A Camel, a Needle, and an Impossible Picture

A camel through THAT?! Impossible!
Jesus used a shocking picture to show how impossible salvation is by human strength or human security.
The disciples asked, “Who then can be saved?” Jesus pointed them back to God.
“With men it is impossible, but not with God: for with God all things are possible.” Mark 10:27
4
Reliance

What Are You Leaning On?

Lesson 4 uses the word reliance: what do we depend on? What gives us “confident rest for support”?
The question is not: “Do I have money?”
The question is: “What do I rely on?”
5
Jesus’ Parable

The Steward Who Wasted His Master’s Money

Luke 16:1–3
Give an account of your stewardship!
Uh-oh. He’s going to check the books!
A wealthy man discovered that his steward had been wasting or mismanaging his resources and demanded an account.
What shall I do? I’m losing my stewardship!
Instead of becoming responsible, the dishonest steward began scheming.
6
The Scheme

He Changed the Bills!

Write fifty!
Write eighty!
He cut the debts so that, after losing his job, the debtors might help him later.
Important: The dishonesty is still wrong. The lesson does not present stealing as good. The surprising point comes next: the master noticed that the steward had finally started thinking about the future.
7
The Surprise

Why Was the Dishonest Steward Commended?

Luke says the master commended the unjust steward “because he had done wisely.” Lesson 4 interprets the wisdom as recognizing money’s ability to affect the future.
This becomes one of the chapter’s two main conclusions.
“And the lord commended the unjust steward, because he had done wisely...” Luke 16:8
8
Hidden Treasure

The Golden Egg Is Not the Greatest Treasure

The lesson uses the old golden-egg picture: an egg is valuable, but the source producing the eggs is even more valuable.
Lesson 4 says money is not the true asset; God’s blessing and favor are the deeper treasure.
“...it is he that giveth thee power to get wealth...” Deuteronomy 8:18
Hidden treasure: Don’t become so fascinated with the “golden eggs” that you forget the One you believe is the source of every good thing.
9
Future Thinking

Spend Only for Today—or Invest in Tomorrow?

Lesson 4 warns against using everything on short-lived pleasures and ignoring the future.
A steward plans for later years, family needs, and ways to help people—not merely today’s wants.
Future thinking includes both: responsible provision for your household and using resources to touch other people’s lives.
10
Eternal Treasure

Money Will Not Last—People Matter Forever

Things wear out. People matter.
So money can become “eternal treasure” when it helps people and the Gospel.
Lesson 4 says money itself does not go to heaven, but resources can be used in ways that have lasting effects on people.
This is the chapter’s strongest practical priority.
“...Make to yourselves friends of the mammon of unrighteousness; that, when ye fail, they may receive you into everlasting habitations.” Luke 16:9
11
Balance

Giving Does Not Mean Neglecting Your Family

The source lesson explicitly says a person is not a good steward if the family is left without basic shelter because everything was given away.
Stewardship needs balance: take care of real needs, plan wisely, and still ask how much of what God gives you can be used to bless others.
Not stewardship:
“I will ignore my responsibilities so I can say I gave everything.”
Stewardship:
“I will care for my responsibilities and still live with an eternal perspective.”
12
The Big Idea

Two Lessons From the Dishonest Steward

The chapter says God’s blessing and favor are more important than money itself.
Money should not all disappear into short-lived pleasures; some should help shape a wise and generous future.
Chapter 4 in one sentence:
Hidden treasure is found when we stop trusting money as our security and start using it as a tool—caring for real needs, planning for the future, and investing in people and God’s purposes.
