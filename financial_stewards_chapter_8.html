FINANCIAL STEWARDS • CHAPTER 8
Scene 1

Where Should We Give?

I know giving matters—but where should it go?
Lesson 8 begins with a practical stewardship question: where should tithes and offerings be given, and how should those resources be used?
The lesson interprets “storehouse” as the place where you receive spiritual nourishment.
“Bring ye all the tithes into the storehouse...” Malachi 3:10
2
A Healthy Church

A Good Church Does More Than Preach

Lesson 8 says a good local church teaches Scripture, builds community, helps children grow, offers counsel, supports people through hard times, and helps widows and orphans.
The lesson's conclusion: if your local church is healthy, teaching the Word, and serving people, it should be a primary place for your giving.
3
Planting

Good Soil or Pavement?

The lesson says it matters where you “plant” your giving.
Supporting a fruitful church or ministry helps support the work it actually does.
Stewardship includes discernment: giving is also support for the conduct, message, and mission of the organization receiving it.
4
Support What You Value

Giving Is a Kind of Vote

Lesson 8 compares receiving nourishment from one place while financially supporting another place you do not agree with to eating at one restaurant and paying a different restaurant.
Child-friendly takeaway: support ministries and causes whose teaching and actions you genuinely believe are doing good.
5
Different Kinds of Giving

Not Every Gift Has the Same Purpose

The lesson treats this as the primary place for regular giving when the church is healthy.
Some giving goes directly toward caring for people in need.
The lesson also identifies missionary giving as a valid use of offerings.
6
Watch for Pressure

“Send Money or You’ll Miss Your Miracle!”

Lesson 8 tells a story about a personalized fundraising letter that promised salvation, healing, and prosperity in exchange for money.
The lesson condemns emotional coercion and desperation-based giving.
Important: Emotion itself is not bad. The lesson says it is okay to be moved emotionally—but emotion should not replace wisdom, truth, and a willing heart.
7
Bible Adventure

Simon Tries to Buy God's Gift

Give me this power too!
You thought the gift of God could be purchased with money!
In Acts 8, Simon offered money in exchange for spiritual power. Peter sharply rebuked him.
This is the chapter's clearest protection against transactional giving.
“Thy money perish with thee, because thou hast thought that the gift of God may be purchased with money.” Acts 8:20
8
Faith vs. Purchase

Giving May Be an Act of Faith—But It Is Not a Purchase

Buying idea:
“I gave money, so God now owes me a miracle.”
Faith idea:
“I trust God enough to obey and give freely, without trying to purchase His power.”
Lesson 8 explicitly says giving itself does not cause a miracle; it may sometimes be an expression of faith.
9
Integrity

Honest Ministry Matters

Lesson 8 includes examples of fundraising built on false emergencies and misleading claims.
The lesson places integrity above raising more money.
For kids: A good cause should not need lies to make people generous.
10
How to Decide

A Steward's Giving Checklist

This checklist translates Lesson 8's principles into decisions children can understand with help from parents and church leaders.
11
The Big Idea

Give Where Good Things Are Growing

That is the strongest visual summary of Lesson 8.
Chapter 8 in one sentence:
Give thoughtfully to healthy churches, ministries, missions, and people in need; support work that truly feeds and serves, and never let fear, manipulation, or promises of purchased miracles control your giving.
12
Steward Challenge

Talk, Think & Do

🧠 Think About It

1. How does Lesson 8 explain the “storehouse”?
2. What are some things a healthy local church does?
3. Why does it matter where you give?
4. What is benevolence giving? What is missionary giving?
5. What warning signs of manipulation appear in this lesson?
6. What did Simon try to buy in Acts 8?
7. Can money purchase a miracle or a gift from God?

🌱 Good-Soil Challenge

With a parent or church teacher, choose one church ministry, missionary effort, or helping project. Ask what it actually does and who it helps. Then decide whether you would feel good supporting it and why.

“God, help me give wisely and support what is good.”
