FINANCIAL STEWARDS • CHAPTER 5
Scene 1

Two Goals Pulling at Once

Can both be my number one goal?
Lesson 5 begins with focus. A divided heart cannot make God’s kingdom first while also making getting rich the main goal.
The chapter connects this to Paul’s words, “This one thing I do.”
Main idea: Prosperity is presented as a byproduct of seeking God—not the goal we chase.
2
Single Vision

Keep Your Eye on One Thing

Can I work, study, plan, and still keep God first?
Yes. Ordinary life can still be centered on God.
Matthew 6:22–23 is used as a picture of undivided focus.
“The light of the body is the eye: if therefore thine eye be single, thy whole body shall be full of light.” Matthew 6:22
3
A New Reason to Work

Work So You Can Give

I thought work was just to pay bills!
Lesson 5 gives work a bigger purpose: honest work can produce resources that help other people.
Every resource received has the potential to influence someone’s life for the better.
“...let him labour, working with his hands the thing which is good, that he may have to give to him that needeth.” Ephesians 4:28
4
Two Hands

One Hand Receives. One Hand Gives.

God can put resources into my hand...
...so they can flow through me to bless others.
The lesson uses this two-hands picture: one hand receives and the other gives.
“...always having all sufficiency in all things, may abound to every good work.” 2 Corinthians 9:8
The point: Prosperity in this lesson is measured by how much blessing can flow through a person—not merely how much that person can collect.
5
Firstfruits

First—Not Leftovers

Lesson 5 contrasts firstfruits with giving God what is left after everything else.
This is about priority and heart posture.
“Honour the LORD with thy substance, and with the firstfruits of all thine increase...” Proverbs 3:9
6
Bible Adventure

Elijah and the Widow

I only have a handful of meal and a little oil...
Do not fear. The meal shall not waste, neither shall the oil fail.
During a severe drought, a widow had almost nothing left. Elijah gave her a promise from God.
The lesson says she acted on the promise, and God sustained her household through the drought.
“The barrel of meal shall not waste, neither shall the cruse of oil fail...” 1 Kings 17:14
For children: The emphasis is the widow’s trust in God’s promise. This story should never be used to pressure people in crisis to surrender necessities to prove their faith.
7
Jesus Teaches

Look at the Birds and Lilies

Birds don’t spend their lives panicking about tomorrow.
Jesus used birds as a picture of the Father’s care.
And the lilies don’t make their beauty happen by worrying!
Jesus also pointed to flowers as a reminder that the Father knows what people need.
8
Matthew 6

God Knows What You Need

In the lesson’s reading of Matthew 6, Jesus is specifically talking about everyday needs such as food, clothing, and where people live.
“...your heavenly Father knoweth that ye have need of all these things.” Matthew 6:32
The response Jesus gives is not “panic harder.” It is to put God’s kingdom first and trust the Father with your needs.
9
Saving Wisely

Can You Save and Still Seek God First?

Lesson 5 warns against treating savings as ultimate security.
The same lesson also says saving can be good when it prepares for future needs, generosity, and inheritance.
“A good man leaveth an inheritance to his children’s children...” Proverbs 13:22
Balance: Seeking God first does not mean refusing to plan. It means planning without making money your master.
10
Not a Formula

Kingdom First Is a Heart Condition

The lesson explicitly says this cannot be reduced to a get-rich formula.
The issue must become a heart-level revelation, not merely outward motions.
For children: Giving is never a trick to make God hand us more stuff. The chapter is about priority, trust, generosity, and peace.
11
The Climax

Seek First the Kingdom

So I still work...
...still plan wisely...
...but God stays first!
Exactly. The lesson does not tell people to abandon responsibility. It tells them not to make material provision the center of life.
“But seek ye first the kingdom of God, and his righteousness; and all these things shall be added unto you.” Matthew 6:33
Chapter 5 in one sentence:
Seek God first, work faithfully, give generously, plan wisely, and let prosperity be a byproduct of a kingdom-centered life rather than the thing your heart chases.
12
Steward Challenge

Talk, Think & Do

🧠 Think About It

1. What does it mean to have a “single” focus?
2. According to Ephesians 4:28, what is one reason to work?
3. What does the “two hands” picture mean?
4. Why are firstfruits different from leftovers?
5. What did God promise the widow through Elijah?
6. Can a person save money and still seek God first? Explain.
7. Why is “seek first the kingdom” not a get-rich formula?

👑 Kingdom-First Challenge

Before you spend your next allowance or gift, stop and ask: “How can I put God first with what I have?” You might give some, save some, help someone, or make a wiser spending choice.

“God, help me put Your kingdom first.”
